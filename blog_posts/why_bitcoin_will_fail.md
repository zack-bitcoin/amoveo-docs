Why Bitcoin Will Fail
================

Economics has tools to tell you what an asset will be worth. These tools tell us that, in the long run, bitcoin will be worth almost nothing.

The efficient market hypothesis can be summarized as: any investment is as good as any other.
Imagine for example, that one kind of investment, say, beanie babies, was better than the rest of the investments.
That means that beanie babies are underpriced in comparison to what they are worth.
In that case, investors would keep selling other assets to buy more beanie babies, which would cause the price of beanie babies to increase.
The price of beanie babies would keep increasing, until beanie babies become as good as an investment as everything else.

The market has a certain interest rate = R.
Assets that pay out less than the interest rate tend to decrease in price, until they pay out the interest rate.
Assets that pay out more than the interest rate tend to increase in price, until they pay out the interest rate.

So, how does this apply to bitcoin?
In Bitcoin, the holders of BTC get paid when fees are paid to miners. If $100 is paid in miner fees, and you own 2% of the BTC, then it is like you received $2 of that transaction fee.

So, if we apply the efficient market hypothesis to bitcoin to try and predict the long term equilibrium market cap of bitcoin, what do we get?
The market cap of bitcoin is going to keep adjusting until (fees paid)/(market cap) = R.

Historically, R tends to be around 5%.
Expecting the bitcoin network to pay 5% of the bitcoin annually as fees is absurd.
Most money doesn't even move in a given year, and so pays zero fees.

As the price of BTC keeps falling, the fees will get lower at the same time. Because there is less money available to make transactions.
So, bitcoin's price will eventually enter a death spiral and never recover.

Applying the same logic to Fiat currencies
===================

Governments collect taxes. When part of the money is soaked up as taxes, this makes the rest of the money valuable.
(taxes paid)/(market cap) = R
 -> (market cap) = R*(taxes paid)

The more taxes that the government collects, the bigger the market cap of their currency can be.
It is reasonable for a government to collect 5% of the fiat currency in taxes in a single year.

How can cryptocurrency overcome this?
===================

A land registry blockchain could own the land in the registry. This way the market cap is worth as much as all the land in the registry.

The blockchain doesen't need physical control over the land. Instead it taxes proportional to the rent on that land.
